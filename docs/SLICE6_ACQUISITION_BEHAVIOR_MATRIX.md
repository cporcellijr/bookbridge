# Slice 6 acquisition behavior matrix

Source checkpoint: `629ebad0` (slice 5 complete). Static review of the three files
below; no acquisition, provider call, or behavior change was performed.

BookOrbit has two roles here: request provider and reader library/scan server.
Shelfmark is an acquisition provider. A Shelfmark delivery can subsequently appear
in a reader's BookOrbit library; that does not make the provider protocols equal.
`search_reader_library` enumerates library sources, not Shelfmark activity
(`src/services/acquisition_library_catalog.py:55-60`, `:110-117`).

## Legacy receipt paths are a separate boundary

Ungrouped/legacy receipts use `_on_available` for BookOrbit and `_on_downloaded`
for Shelfmark (`src/services/acquisition_service.py:1234-1252`, `:1206-1230`).
They can converge on `_resolve_arrival` (`:1254-1276`), but its `client` is the
BookOrbit library client even when the receipt originated at Shelfmark. Shelfmark
also tries an ebook filename lookup first. These paths do not have the same proof
contract as durable-job `_candidate_matches`/`_record_bookorbit_job_arrival`.
`_unresolved` (`:1278-1292`) can write/review/refuse; it is not a pure normalizer.

## Slice 6 boundary

The truly identical rows already have shared implementations: durable intent,
catalog matching/review, progress-mark tracking and final reconciliation/pairing.
There is no additional whole provider lifecycle proven equivalent by this review.
Do not merge status adapters, receipt constructors, release selectors or scan
retry based on superficial structural similarity. Any narrower future extraction
needs before/after evidence for missing metadata, partial submissions, uncertain
acknowledgements, late receipts and reader-scoped catalog failures.
